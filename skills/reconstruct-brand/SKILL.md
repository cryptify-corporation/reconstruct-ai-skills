# Establish the product's own identity

Determine whether the user wants a new product identity, a rebrand or faithful restoration of their own interface. Preserve an established identity unless changing it is requested. Brand work is not a mandatory stage for every Reconstruct task.

## Define the direction

Use the intended audience and evidenced positioning from existing work. If research is absent, label the positioning as proposed rather than manufacturing support. Translate it into a naming direction, visual roles and tone of voice suited to the product.

Generate a focused set of candidates and compare clarity, pronunciation, audience fit and similarity to existing products. When checking domains, public product names or marks, use current primary sources and record the date and exact result. Distinguish a preliminary search from legal clearance; do not claim availability from an unperformed check.

## Apply identity

Map the selected palette into the existing token roles and verify important text/background and focus combinations. Do not change colors merely to satisfy a blanket rule if they belong to the user's existing brand. Design logo requirements around actual placements and sizes; create deliverables only with available authorized tools.

Define concise voice guidance and rewrite the product's own high-visibility strings when requested. For a new product based on another product's patterns, use its own or licensed logos, assets and content. Inspect titles, favicons, metadata, empty states and emails for unintended borrowed identity.

Search reference names and domains as a diagnostic, then inspect matches. Preserve legitimate attribution, integration identifiers and documented comparisons; substring matches alone do not justify deleting them. A clean text search is not proof that all visual assets are appropriate.

## Deliver

Provide the chosen direction, candidates or implemented identity, tokens, asset provenance, copy examples and dated checks still needed. Do not buy domains, publish campaigns or represent uncertain trademark questions as settled. Keep naming, design and implementation changes within the user's requested scope.
